# Cercospora leaf spot on okra
Topic: disease

## What it looks like
Cercospora leaf spot starts on the older, lower leaves of okra. It shows as brown, roughly angular spots and dark olive to sooty mould patches, mainly on the underside of the leaf. Affected leaves turn yellow and fall early, which weakens the plant and lowers pod yield. The Crop Health leaf scan labels it "Cercospora Leaf Spot".

## Conditions that favour it
Cercospora leaf spot is a fungal disease favoured by warm (about 25–30 °C), humid, rainy weather. Spores spread by rain splash, wind, and on hands and tools, and the fungus survives on old crop debris in the soil. Outdoor fields after several wet days are most at risk; dense indoor plantings with high humidity can also be affected.

## What to do today
Pick off and remove spotted lower leaves and take them away from the field. Avoid working among wet plants, which spreads spores. Improve airflow by removing crowded lower leaves, and water at the base, not over the leaves.

## Prevention
Remove old okra debris after each crop, rotate the field away from okra and other mallow-family crops for at least one season, keep weeds down, and space plants for airflow. Balanced fertilising keeps plants strong; avoid excess nitrogen, which makes soft, dense growth.

## Chemical control
If Cercospora leaf spot spreads to the upper canopy, ask JA or the agricultural extension office which registered fungicides are allowed for okra, and follow the label and pre-harvest interval.
