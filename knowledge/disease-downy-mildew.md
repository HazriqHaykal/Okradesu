# Downy mildew on okra
Topic: disease

## What it looks like
Downy mildew shows as pale yellow, angular patches on the upper side of okra leaves, bounded by the leaf veins. On the underside of the same patches a grey to purple, fuzzy growth appears, most visible in the early morning. Patches turn brown and dry, and badly affected leaves drop. The Crop Health leaf scan labels it "Downy Mildew".

## Conditions that favour it
Downy mildew is caused by a water mould, not a true fungus. It needs leaves that stay wet or humidity above about 85% for several hours, and it spreads fastest in mild temperatures (roughly 15–22 °C) with cool, damp nights. Spores travel on wind and water splash. Indoor rooms with humidity above 75% overnight and little airflow are at risk.

## What to do today
Lower leaf wetness and humidity first: run the fans (the app's rule is 20 minutes every hour while humidity is above 75%), keep humidity in the 60–75% target, and water at the base of the plant in the morning so leaves dry quickly. Remove and bag the worst leaves; do not leave them on the floor. Space plants or prune crowded leaves to open the canopy.

## Prevention
Avoid overhead watering, keep plant spacing generous, clear fallen leaves every week, and check the undersides of leaves when humidity has been high overnight. Choose resistant varieties where available.

## Chemical control
If downy mildew keeps spreading after airflow and hygiene are fixed, ask JA or the agricultural extension office which registered products are allowed for okra in Japan, and follow the label and pre-harvest interval exactly.
