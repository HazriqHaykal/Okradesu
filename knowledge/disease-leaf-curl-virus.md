# Okra leaf curl and yellow vein virus
Topic: disease

## What it looks like
Virus-infected okra plants have leaves that curl upward or downward, thickened and twisted veins, and often a yellow network along the veins (yellow vein mosaic). Plants become stunted and produce few, small, pale or misshapen pods. The Crop Health leaf scan labels leaf curl as "Leaf Curly Virus".

## How it spreads
These okra viruses are spread by whiteflies feeding on the plants. They are not spread by water or soil, and an infected plant cannot be cured. Warm weather with many whiteflies raises the risk, and weeds nearby can host both the whiteflies and the virus.

## What to do today
Pull out clearly infected okra plants, bag them and remove them from the farm so whiteflies cannot carry the virus to healthy plants. Check the undersides of nearby leaves for whiteflies and hang yellow sticky traps to monitor them. Note the row, since the harvest from those plants is lost.

## Prevention
Keep insect-proof netting on the vents and doors of indoor rooms, control weeds around the fields, start with healthy seedlings, and use tolerant or resistant varieties where available. Inspect new plants before bringing them into an indoor room.

## Whitefly control
Use sticky traps, netting and natural enemies first. If whitefly numbers stay high, ask JA or the agricultural extension office which registered measures are allowed for okra.
