# Powdery mildew on okra
Topic: disease

## What it looks like
Powdery mildew appears as white to grey, powdery spots on okra leaves, usually on the upper surface first, which join up until the leaf looks dusted with flour. Leaves yellow, dry and drop early, and pod yield falls.

## Conditions that favour it
Unlike downy mildew, powdery mildew does not need wet leaves. It spreads with warm days and cooler nights, moderate to high humidity (often 60–85%), and a dense, shaded canopy with weak airflow. Indoor rooms with stagnant air and humidity staying above 75% overnight are typical. Leaf temperatures around 24–27 °C suit the spores.

## What to do today
Run the fans to move air through the canopy and bring humidity back into the 60–75% target, remove the most affected leaves, and thin crowded growth. Check whether any LED row is weaker than the others, since weak, shaded plants are affected first.

## Prevention
Keep good spacing and airflow, avoid excess nitrogen fertiliser, remove old leaves regularly, and watch the overnight humidity trend, not only daytime readings.

## Chemical control
If powdery mildew spreads despite airflow and leaf removal, ask JA or the agricultural extension office which registered products are allowed for okra, and follow the label and pre-harvest interval.
