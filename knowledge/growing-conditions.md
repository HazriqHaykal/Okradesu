# Okra growing conditions and targets
Topic: growing

## Temperature
Okra is a warm-season crop. It grows best with air at 24–30 °C. Below about 18 °C growth slows, below 15 °C it nearly stops and pods set poorly, and near 10 °C plants are damaged. Above about 35 °C flowers and young pods can drop and pods toughen quickly (heat stress). Warmer weather also makes pods ready sooner after flowering.

## Soil moisture and watering
The soil-moisture target for okra is 35–45%. Below 30% the plant is stressed: water deeply at the base, early in the day (the pump rule waters 20 seconds when soil is under 30%). Above 60% the soil is waterlogged and roots can rot, so skip watering and check drainage. Deep, less frequent watering is better than small daily sprinkles, and outdoor fields should skip watering when rain is forecast.

## Soil pH and nutrients
Okra prefers soil pH 6.0–6.8. Below 5.5 the soil is too acidic (lime may be needed, after a soil test); above 7.5 it is too alkaline and nutrients lock up. Nutrient strength (EC) should stay about 1.2–2.0 mS/cm: below 0.8 add fertiliser, above 2.8 flush with plain water. Too much nitrogen gives leafy plants with fewer pods and more disease.

## Humidity
Keep humidity at 60–75% for okra. Above 75% for hours, especially overnight, fungal diseases such as mildew spread, so run the fans. Below 40% the air is very dry and spider mites increase.

## Light
Outdoor okra needs full sun. Indoor rooms use LEDs at about 350–500 µmol of light for 14 hours a day, run from 20:00 to 10:00 on cheaper night-rate electricity. A row reading much lower than the others (for example 250 against 420) usually means a failed or dirty LED strip.
